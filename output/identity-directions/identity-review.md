# Blur Design — identity research and directions
20 October 2026 · Phase 1 + Phase 2 · Awaiting direction selection

**Recommendation: 01 / Clear Cut.** Let the existing blackletter signature supply character, while typography, information hierarchy and interaction demonstrate clarity. The site should first explain the studio, then reward attention.

## Scope and evidence

The six supplied homepages were visited in a desktop browser, scrolled through their page sequences to the footer, and captured in viewport screenshots during this session. Computed CSS supplied font-family names and selected sizes at an approximately 1280px-wide viewport. These are declared font names, not a forensic identification of proprietary font files. Ratios below describe the visual impression of studio-owned surfaces, excluding client artwork; they are estimates, not measured pixel coverage.

Motion notes reflect visible changes during scroll and repeated captures. Hover notes explicitly marked **CSS** come from declared stylesheet rules, not a complete pointer-interaction test. Some animated sections were briefly blank during rapid captures; this limits assessment of transition details. No frame-accurate timing, mobile-motion or reduced-motion audit was performed.

Refero styles were searched across monochrome studio, experimental agency, known studio, agency and minimalist queries; all returned no results. Refero screens and Mobbin were also searched. Results were mainly unrelated product screens and site-builder examples, so they were rejected as evidence of established studio taste. Five additional real studios were reviewed directly instead. No Refero-extracted style specification is claimed.

The supplied Figma node [53:588](https://www.figma.com/design/TPJOv3OOOfSabSxcu7b00O/Blur-Landing-Page?node-id=53-588) was inspected through a screenshot. The console bridge was disconnected and a full file-tree read was unavailable. **No Figma page, node, text style, variable or token was created or changed.** Directions are supplied as generated preview images. Their font and logo rendering is approximate; the existing vector artwork remains authoritative.

## The logo as the starting point

The logo contains a dense blackletter B, flowing bowls and counters, angular terminals, and a small diamond detail. The full wordmark has the same heavy, pointed character and diamond punctuation.

This already provides enough expression. Repeating gothic forms in headlines and body copy would reduce legibility and risk pushing the identity toward fashion, music, gaming or a guild aesthetic. Use the original mark intact as a signature. Borrow its contrast between curves and cuts in composition, and its diamond sparingly as a chapter or navigation detail. Do not turn the diamond into a repetitive decorative pattern or assume a generic 45-degree angle precisely matches the source geometry.

## Phase 1 — supplied references

### 1. Unikorns
[Reference](https://unikorns.agency/)

- **Type:** CSS declares Aeonik. A sampled hero is about 76px / weight 500 / −0.02em tracking; introductory copy about 21px with similarly tight tracking. Sentence case and large-to-small contrast organize a spacious split composition. Small navigation is much quieter.
- **Color:** White, black and grey control most interface surfaces, roughly 90–95% neutral in the shell. Blue/purple atmosphere and vivid project imagery create concentrated changes of mood. Light presentation dominates; dark media intervals punctuate it.
- **Layout:** Wide margins, left-aligned major headings, offset supporting copy and large project media. Service divisions and project captions keep the portfolio legible. Rounded containers soften otherwise systematic alignment.
- **Motion:** Scroll introduces changing media and text states; the team/play area makes exploration a distinct chapter. **CSS:** circular action masks expand, letters shift on link hover, and paired arrows translate diagonally. Satisfaction comes from consistent small feedback around a quiet structure.
- **One idea to adapt:** Separate the simple studio explanation from richer project storytelling.
- **Avoid for Blur:** The extensive agency-scale play area and generic creative/development positioning. Blur should state brand identity, product UI and design elevation accurately.

### 2. Nothin’
[Reference](https://www.noth.in/)

- **Type:** CSS declares PP Neue Montreal, with IBM Plex Mono for some project labels. Sampled statements are about 64px / −0.01em; uppercase micro-labels around 10px use expanded tracking. A huge wordmark and sparse statements carry the composition.
- **Color:** Strong black/white transitions dominate the structural palette, roughly 85–90% neutral outside imagery. Metallic blue, pink and silver objects provide deliberate interruptions.
- **Layout:** Large empty intervals, full-width chapters, a prominent work sequence and unusual image cutouts. The layout often behaves like a sequence of staged scenes.
- **Motion:** A counter-based opening, animated object scenes, changing wordmark scale and immersive media make the page theatrical. **CSS:** buttons change their border treatment on hover. Exact pointer choreography was not verified.
- **One idea to adapt:** Give a single clear proposition enough space before introducing surprise.
- **Avoid for Blur:** Long entrance gates and shiny floating objects. The metaphor can become more memorable than the studio’s services and can edge toward familiar digital/crypto imagery.

### 3. Kins
[Reference](https://bykins.com/)

- **Type:** CSS declares Manuka Black for a sampled 130px display statement, with Geist for navigation and prose. A sampled introduction is 30px, light, with −0.02em tracking; navigation is 14px with slight positive tracking. The distinction between condensed display and open text creates clear roles.
- **Color:** The information layer is predominantly white and black, approximately 85–90% neutral excluding project assets. Iridescent cyan/chrome typography and grey gradient surfaces supply brand spectacle.
- **Layout:** A dramatic opening gives way to repeated text-and-image project rows, dividers and service explanations. A persistent curved navigation treatment stays visible.
- **Motion:** Repeated scroll reveals and changing emphasis in business-problem statements create progression. The reflective hero material and pointer-adjacent details add energy. Framework hover rules were present, but custom hover behavior was not independently established.
- **One idea to adapt:** Connect project imagery to a concise client problem and design response.
- **Avoid for Blur:** Chrome, glossy navigation and dominance-oriented copy. These fight the understated brief and increase Web3 associations.

### 4. Lift Type
[Reference](https://www.lift-type.fr/)

- **Type:** CSS declares Dwight for the site interface; navigation sampled around 16px / −0.01em. Specimens intentionally use many of the foundry’s own families. Large changes of width, case and weight are the content rather than one consistent identity treatment.
- **Color:** Neutral navigation surrounds intensely colored specimen panels: red, violet, green, blue, pink and more. This is the major palette outlier. A rough structural impression is closer to half neutral / half saturated content, changing with the viewport.
- **Layout:** A stable two-column specimen catalogue, consistent captions and useful controls let very different typefaces coexist. In-use media provides evidence beyond alphabet samples.
- **Motion:** Specimen slides, alternate views and a type tester encourage direct comparison. The satisfaction comes from manipulating real typographic content rather than waiting through a cinematic sequence.
- **One idea to adapt:** Show the same meaningful phrase across sizes and applications so the identity proves its range.
- **Avoid for Blur:** A different font and palette in every section. Lift sells typographic variety; Blur needs one recognizable system.

### 5. Poch
[Reference](https://poch.studio/)

- **Type:** CSS declares Season Mix Regular for a sampled 50px introduction and 25px project headings; Season Sans SemiBold for small descriptions and labels; Lacquer for hand-drawn section labels and actions. Mostly sentence case, little declared tracking, with warmth coming from mixed letterforms and hand-made interruptions.
- **Color:** A black/white foundation dominates the shell, around 85–90% neutral outside the collage. Pink, green, blue and a pale yellow action create playful relief.
- **Layout:** A centered statement, surreal object collage, floating bottom navigation, substantial project explanations, service blocks and package cards. The dense commercial information is divided into readable stages.
- **Motion:** Letter-level blur/reveal is visible in some headings and footer links; animated media and playful scale changes keep the black canvas lively. The floating navigation maintains orientation. Custom hover details were not fully verified.
- **One idea to adapt:** Make contact and service explanations approachable through plain language and visible people.
- **Avoid for Blur:** Surreal prop accumulation, hand-drawn labels everywhere, and essential text that stays blurred while the visitor scrolls. The cumulative tone is more playful than the brief.

### 6. Haus
[Reference](https://madeinhaus.com/)

- **Type:** CSS declares internal families named monument, monument_mono and haus_headline. These names do not establish a retail font purchase. Main prose sampled around 42px; large headline compositions around 169–223px. Uppercase display forms contrast with sentence-case prose and small mono metadata.
- **Color:** Yellow, peach/orange and near-white chapter fields with dark type. A bright orange footer is a deliberate ending. Color occupies entire scenes, so it is a second major outlier against a neutral-first preference.
- **Layout:** Viewport-sized scenes, highly visible navigation, central cinematic media, oversized lettering and generous spacing. Project images interrupt large display words rather than sitting inside a conventional card grid.
- **Motion:** Letter substitutions and scene changes are directly visible; media and typography trade prominence as the visitor scrolls. **CSS:** underlines retract, some containers round on hover, italic animation switches, and the logo changes its fill treatment.
- **One idea to adapt:** Design a deliberate rhythm of statement → evidence → explanation.
- **Avoid for Blur:** Constant letter substitution, multiple novelty display forms and full-page saturated fields. Haus is a useful upper limit for expression, not the default level for Blur.

## Synthesis

### Likely real taste

The common thread is strong authorship held inside an understandable structure:

1. Typography creates the layout rather than merely filling it.
2. Neutral interface surfaces give expressive work room to breathe.
3. A large statement is balanced by quiet, precise supporting information.
4. Project media receives enough space to matter.
5. Motion changes emphasis and pace instead of merely fading in every element.
6. A small number of memorable gestures is more compelling than constant decoration.

These are interpretations of the reference set, not claims about the user beyond the brief.

### Outliers

- **Lift Type** pulls toward experimentation and tactile typographic comparison.
- **Poch** pulls toward friendly, eccentric studio personality.
- **Haus** pulls toward cinematic production and a more colorful public face.
- **Nothin’ and Kins** introduce glossy material spectacle, which is the least useful ingredient for Blur’s positioning.

### Tensions to resolve

| Tension | Recommended resolution |
|---|---|
| Expressive blackletter vs clarity | Preserve the logo; use a highly legible supporting type system. |
| Playful vs restrained | One optional surprise per chapter, after the message is understood. |
| Type-led vs image-led | Lead with the studio proposition, then let real project evidence carry attention. |
| General studio vs Web3 portfolio | Describe the design problem and outcome; keep sector labels as metadata. |
| Satisfying scroll vs easy reading | Motion may enrich media and transitions; all essential copy must remain readable. |
| Personality vs consistency | Repeat one recognizable gesture, not a new trick for every section. |

The clearest positioning is: **Blur brings reasons, structure and distinction to brands and digital products.** Do not sell development or imply that all client work is outside fintech. A general studio position comes from transferable expertise and varied presentation, supported by the actual portfolio.

## Phase 2 — three distinct directions

All values below are **proposed swatches and type choices**, not implemented tokens. No primitive ramps, semantic mappings, text styles or variable collections have been built.

### 01 / Clear Cut — recommended

**Concept:** Every visible choice has a clear reason.

**Primary reference:** Unikorns owns the spacious canvas and typographic hierarchy. Borrow only Lift Type’s practice of proving typography through specimens and Order’s restrained project metadata.

**Logo connection:** Its dark, carved silhouette is the signature against a quiet white field. Pair flowing blackletter curves with an upright geometric sans, not more gothic lettering. A single diamond can signal a chapter or action. Preserve the logo intact; any future geometric extraction needs the source vectors.

**Type:** Manrope Medium/Semibold for display + DM Sans Regular/Medium for text. Starting study: 88px hero at desktop / 46px mobile; 18px prose; tight but readable display leading, normal text leading. Use sentence case, approximately −0.03em display tracking and normal body tracking. These are sample art-direction settings, not a committed scale.

**Licensing:** Both families are available under SIL OFL 1.1. Font license cost **₱0** for the proposed commercial web use, with license notices retained when distributing fonts. Sources: [Manrope OFL](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/manrope/OFL.txt), [DM Sans OFL](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/dmsans/OFL.txt).

**Color:** Paper #FAFAF8; ink #151515; secondary grey #555555; cobalt #2448D8. Approximately 95% neutral / up to 5% accent in studio-owned UI. Cobalt identifies actions or an active chapter, not decorative hero atmosphere.

**Main contrast checks:** Ink/paper 17.47:1; grey/paper 7.13:1; white/cobalt 7.02:1; cobalt/paper 6.71:1.

**Layout and motion principles:**
- Use a broad 12-column desktop composition, an asymmetric proposition/supporting-copy split, and generous vertical intervals; reflow to a single clear reading sequence on mobile.
- Keep service rows and project captions visible and aligned; square or minimally rounded media.
- A case-study beat should explain the problem, show the design choice, then state why it helps. Use only supported outcomes.
- Animate a single media crop or diamond transition per chapter; proposed small-feedback duration 160–220ms and major reveal 500–650ms, without withholding text. Reduced motion shows the final state.

**Stylescape:** [01-clear-cut.png](01-clear-cut.png). Hero: “Good design. Clear reasons.” Type specimen: Manrope / DM Sans. Content section: the three service rows.

**Risks:** White + grotesk + cobalt can become a generic digital agency. The existing logo, clear rationale captions and one consistent diamond gesture must remain the recognizable traits. Cobalt must not spread into glows or gradient backgrounds, which would increase fintech/crypto associations.

### 02 / The Rationale

**Concept:** Make the thinking visible, so the design becomes easier to trust.

**Primary reference:** Lift Type owns the specimen and comparison logic. Borrow only COLLINS’ restrained whole-headline serif approach and Poch’s approachable explanations.

**Logo connection:** Treat the wordmark like an editorial masthead. The logo’s contrast and cut terminals relate to a reading-oriented serif, while a modern sans and strict annotation grid keep it contemporary. The mark remains a signature, not a repeated crest.

**Type:** Newsreader Medium for full display headlines + DM Sans Regular/Medium for explanatory text. Starting study: 80px display desktop / 44px mobile; 18px prose, about 1.55 line height. Upright headings, generous measure, no decorative italic word swaps. Do not reproduce the generated board’s serif rendering of the “DM Sans” label; actual text must use the specified sans family.

**Licensing:** Newsreader and DM Sans use SIL OFL 1.1; proposed font license cost **₱0**. Sources: [Newsreader OFL](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/newsreader/OFL.txt), [DM Sans OFL](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/dmsans/OFL.txt).

**Color:** Cool paper #F3F4F0; charcoal #202420; secondary #536052; dark green #344D3C; pale citron #E6E878. Keep about 90–95% of studio surfaces neutral, dark green for structural emphasis and citron for a small note or selected annotation.

**Main contrast checks:** Charcoal/paper 14.24:1; secondary/paper 6.01:1; white/green 9.24:1; charcoal/citron 12.12:1. Citron is not a text color on paper.

**Layout and motion principles:**
- Use chapter numbers, thin rules and a dedicated annotation gutter; stack annotations after their related content on mobile.
- Keep the hierarchy proposition → evidence → explanation; use a comfortable 55–70-character text measure.
- Show meaningful comparisons, not speculative alternatives passed off as client history.
- Reveal one explanation beside its visual evidence; proposed 250–400ms transitions, with no pinning required for reading. Reduced motion keeps notes expanded.

**Stylescape:** [02-the-rationale.png](02-the-rationale.png). Hero: “Make the reason visible.” Type specimen: Newsreader / DM Sans. Content: Understand → Explore → Explain. The shadow image is conceptual art, not a fabricated client case.

**Risks:** Serif plus blackletter can feel too historical or editorial. Green/citron can pull toward an unrelated sustainability brand if overused. This route also needs strong writing and actual rationale content to avoid becoming a cosmetic publishing theme.

### 03 / Measured Impact

**Concept:** Remove visual noise, then reveal what matters.

**Primary reference:** Haus owns the scene rhythm and the exchange between statement and media. Borrow only Nothin’s contrast between quiet structure and one expressive moment, and MOUTHWASH’s client-media stage. Reject their more theatrical or glossy ingredients.

**Logo connection:** The B’s curves, heavy/light relationship and diamond counter inform a tactile, matte reveal vocabulary. Keep the wordmark small and intact; explore its negative space in media crops rather than warping the original logo. Source geometry is required before making any final mask.

**Type:** Archivo Bold for display + IBM Plex Sans Regular/Medium for text. Starting study: 96px hero desktop / 48px mobile; 18px body. Sentence case with compact but uncramped leading and about −0.02em display tracking. No terminal or mono styling across the interface.

**Licensing:** Both families use SIL OFL 1.1; proposed font license cost **₱0**. Sources: [Archivo OFL](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/archivo/OFL.txt), [IBM Plex Sans OFL](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/ibmplexsans/OFL.txt).

**Color:** Charcoal #171918; chalk #F4F4F0; secondary #B5B9B4; apricot #F3A96B. About 94% neutral / up to 6% accent. Apricot signals action and progress, while project media may retain its own palette.

**Main contrast checks:** Chalk/charcoal 16.02:1; secondary/charcoal 8.89:1; charcoal/apricot 9.00:1; apricot/charcoal 9.00:1.

**Layout and motion principles:**
- Stage one major image at a time, with plain captions and visible context.
- Alternate full media moments with concise explanatory passages; avoid a pinned sequence for every section.
- A single reveal can move from a wider context into a sharply defined detail; never blur service information or navigation.
- Proposed 600–800ms media transitions and 160–220ms interaction feedback; no autoplay audio, with static poster images for reduced motion and mobile where appropriate.

**Stylescape:** [03-measured-impact.png](03-measured-impact.png). Hero: “Less noise. More meaning.” Type specimen: Archivo / IBM Plex Sans. Content: Identity / Product / Elevation. The cut-paper image is exploratory brand imagery, not client evidence.

**Risks:** A dark studio canvas and large type can still look like a crypto or motion studio. Matte materials, warm accent restraint and explicit general-studio services are necessary. This route carries the highest media-production and accessibility burden, and could suggest a motion-heavy offering Blur does not actually sell.

## Contrast method

Ratios were calculated from the exact opaque sRGB swatches using WCAG relative luminance and (Llighter + 0.05) / (Ldarker + 0.05). Every listed main text/button pair exceeds 4.5:1. This does not certify the generated bitmap, thin rules, opacity treatments, text over media, focus states or the future complete system. Those need to be checked after a direction is selected.

## Paid type reference: Lift Type

No paid family is required by these proposals. Dwight is a useful optional benchmark, not a selected substitute.

The [Dwight purchase configurator](https://www.lift-type.fr/products/dwight) displayed advertised starting prices of €50 for a single style, €250 for a width pack, and €500 for the complete family. At the latest published rate visible from the [ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/eurofxref-graph-php.en.html), **€1 = ₱70.715 on 19 October 2026**, those are approximately **₱3,536 / ₱17,679 / ₱35,358**.

These are converted advertised minimums, not a configured quote for Blur: company size and license choice affect the final total; taxes and payment conversion may add cost. No company details were entered, no fonts selected, and no purchase completed.

The current [Lift Type license page](https://www.lift-type.fr/pages/licenses-and-terms) describes Basic as including single-domain web use up to 50,000 monthly page views, while logo/wordmark use requires a separate license. This is another reason to keep the existing logo and use an open-license supporting family for this phase.

## Decision ledger

| Decision | Evidence | Bounded use |
|---|---|---|
| Preserve logo as primary signature | Existing Figma artwork | Keep original curves, terminals and diamond; no logo redesign. |
| Prefer a white, quiet first view | Brief + Unikorns | Establish proposition and navigation before expressive storytelling. |
| Explain project decisions | “Clarity first” brief + Kins’ project descriptions | Plain captions and rationale; no invented impact numbers. |
| Test type with meaningful text | Lift Type | Specimens and comparisons; not a multi-font identity. |
| Keep services explicit | User’s stated offering | Brand identity, product UI, design elevation; no development service. |
| Restrict expressive motion | Brief + Haus/Nothin as upper limits | One purposeful gesture per chapter; readable static baseline. |
| Keep paid type optional | Budget preference + verified OFL sources | All three proposed pairings cost ₱0 in font licensing. |

## Recommendation and stop point

Choose **01 / Clear Cut**. It gives the logo the strongest counterweight, is flexible across fintech and other industries, and turns clarity into an observable behavior: concise propositions, understandable service choices, and project decisions with reasons.

The Rationale is the better alternative if the studio’s distinguishing output will be long-form thinking and case-study explanation. Measured Impact is the more expressive alternative if there is enough strong visual work and a commitment to creating motion assets.

Do not average the three. Clear Cut should retain its white canvas and precise sans hierarchy if selected; borrowing the serif headline, dark shell and cinematic pacing together would dilute the choice.
